Turning Brand Campaigns into a Creator Acquisition Engine

Growth / Product Strategy / Creator Marketplace
ROLE: Product Head PRODUCT: LehLah Creator App + LehLah SaaS PLATFORM: iOS, Android & Web FOCUS: Creator Acquisition, Influencer Marketing & Growth

Overview

LehLah

LehLah is a creator-commerce platform that helps creators monetize their content through affiliate marketing and paid brand collaborations. Creators can generate affiliate links for leading marketplaces including Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, AJIO, Nykaa and Tira, and earn a commission whenever their audience shops through those links.

In 2025, LehLah expanded into influencer marketing. Our affiliate infrastructure gave us an important advantage: instead of reporting only views, reach and engagement, we could also attribute sales generated through creator campaigns.

As more brands began running influencer campaigns through LehLah, we ran into a new problem. We had creators. We didn't always have the right creators.

The Problem

A large creator base didn't necessarily mean a campaign-ready creator base.

LehLah had originally been built for affiliate creators. Our most active creators could drive meaningful GMV, but influencer campaigns required a very different kind of supply. A brand might ask for "female lifestyle creators in Mumbai with 10K+ followers who speak English and create storytelling-led content." Follower count alone couldn't solve that brief. Brands cared about factors such as content quality, niche, location, language, aesthetic, storytelling ability and brand fit. So every time a campaign came in, our team could not rely entirely on creators already available in the app — campaign teams still had to manually discover and reach out to suitable creators.

We needed to acquire the right creators.

We were already spending approximately ₹5L/month on marketing. But increasing ad spend wasn't solving the problem — the issue wasn't simply acquiring more users. It cost us roughly ₹300 to acquire a quality creator who could actually be useful for influencer marketing campaigns, and even then, an ad asking a creator to "Download LehLah" had limited urgency. There was no immediate reason for them to act.

The Insight

What if we stopped acquiring creators first and finding opportunities for them later?

We already had something much more valuable than an advertisement: a real brand collaboration. Creators weren't particularly interested in downloading another creator app, but they were interested in working with brands they knew. That flipped the acquisition problem for us.

Instead of ad → download LehLah → wait for opportunity, we could create relevant brand opportunity → download LehLah → apply. The campaign itself could become our acquisition channel.

The Opportunity

We already had the creators. They just weren't on LehLah yet.

Alongside the creator app, we had built LehLah Discovery, our creator discovery product for brands and internal campaign teams. Discovery contained a much broader database of creators across niches and categories — sourced from publicly available creator information and evaluated using our internal AI-assisted vetting system. This meant we had two creator pools: the LehLah App, where creators had already signed up, and LehLah Discovery, relevant, vetted creators who may not have joined LehLah yet. Paid Collab was originally searching only the first pool. That was the unlock — what if every new campaign searched both?

The Solution

Turn every Paid Collab into a targeted acquisition campaign.

When our team creates a Paid Collab, they already define the creators the brand is looking for — gender, location, follower count, category. Previously, those filters were applied only to creators registered on LehLah. We extended the same matching logic to our Discovery creator database. Now, whenever a campaign is created:

  1. The criteria filters existing LehLah creators.
  2. The same criteria filters relevant creators in Discovery.
  3. Existing creators receive the opportunity through LehLah.
  4. Matching non-LehLah creators with an available contact email receive a personalised collaboration invite.
  5. Interested creators download LehLah to view and apply for the opportunity.

No separate acquisition campaign was required.

The Experience

The message wasn't "Download LehLah to get opportunities."

"LehLah wants to offer you a paid collaboration with Flipkart"

And when applicable, the creator could immediately see the brand associated with the opportunity. That distinction mattered. Instead of asking creators to download an unfamiliar platform and promising that opportunities might appear later, we showed them something valuable before asking them to sign up. The creator journey became: receive invite → see brand opportunity → download LehLah → sign up → view collaboration → apply.

The First Test

Flipkart Minutes gave us the signal we needed.

We tested the flow on a collaboration opportunity for Flipkart Minutes. Instead of relying only on the existing LehLah creator base, matching creators from Discovery also received the collaboration invitation. The response was dramatically stronger than our traditional creator acquisition efforts: 1000+ creator downloads from a single campaign, and a ~70% email open rate. And importantly, these weren't random installs — they were creators who had already matched the campaign criteria and passed through our Discovery vetting process.

Why It Worked

We changed the value exchange.

Traditional acquisition asked the creator to take an action first — download our app and you may find opportunities. This flow gave them the value first — here's an opportunity relevant to you, join LehLah if you want it. That created three advantages.

  • Relevance over reach: campaign filters determined who received the invitation, so we weren't paying to acquire thousands of users and discovering their quality afterward.
  • Intent at the moment of acquisition: creators joined because there was something they already wanted to do inside the product.
  • Brand demand became distribution: every new campaign could now help LehLah acquire the exact type of creators brands were requesting.

The marketplace started helping grow its own supply.

The Flywheel

More campaigns helped us build a better creator network. A better creator network helped us win and execute more campaigns.

Brands bring campaigns → campaign briefs identify the creators we need → Discovery finds relevant creators → creators receive valuable opportunities → high-intent creators join LehLah → LehLah's campaign-ready creator pool improves → future campaigns become easier to fulfil. And the loop repeats.

Impact

The feature started as a relatively small extension to our Paid Collab matching logic. But it changed how we thought about creator acquisition. Instead of spending more money trying to build a generic creator audience, we could use real-time brand demand to determine which creators we should acquire. Over time, this helped diversify LehLah's creator network across formats and creator types, including UGC creators, storytellers, aesthetic creators, affiliate creators and lifestyle creators. Most importantly, this growth did not require us to proportionally increase paid marketing spend.

We weren't just acquiring more creators — we were acquiring creators because brands already needed them.

What I'd Improve

Turn campaign-led acquisition into a continuously expanding creator graph.

The next opportunity would be to increase the breadth and freshness of our Discovery database. Rather than relying only on existing creator records, I'd explore signals that continuously identify creators with proven brand-collaboration experience. For example, we could maintain a list of high-priority D2C and consumer brands and identify creators who publicly collaborate with them. If a creator works with one of those brands, that becomes a useful signal for Discovery: creator collaborates with relevant brand → creator enters discovery pipeline → profile is evaluated → creator becomes eligible for future matching. Over time, this could build a richer creator graph based not only on follower count or category, but on actual collaboration behaviour and brand relevance.

Takeaway

This project reinforced a simple product lesson for me.

Acquisition works differently when the reason to join the product is already waiting on the other side.

We didn't need to convince creators that LehLah might eventually be valuable. We used the value we already had — real brand demand — to give them a reason to join today.
